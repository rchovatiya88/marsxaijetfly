# Camera and mouse: ordinary-input acceptance plan

September 13, 2026. Read-only runtime critique; no gameplay changes made by this report. Target is the dirty `?bridgehead` candidate, with default Ridge Run as a control and `?full-level` retained as a noncombat survey. Freeze and identify a build before recording results. Proposed thresholds below are internal design gates, not validated industry standards.

## Verdict and evidence boundary

The camera math is substantially repaired, but comfortable, understandable flight remains unproved. Do not describe 38 scripted assertions as a playable-route or mouse-feel pass. Human sample size remains **zero**. The reviewer knows and has implemented parts of this runtime, so their checks cannot be called an uncoached fresh-player test.

Confirmed current contracts in `src/components/fly-controls.ts`: the player and boom own yaw only, the camera owns local pitch clamped to ±60 degrees, roll is zero, and weapon aim follows the world camera. Cursor aim requires a canvas-originating left/right drag and uses relative CSS coordinates; pointer lock uses movement deltas. The earlier pitched-bike/orbit defect and hover drift are not current findings. Do not restore smoothing that adds trailing mouse drift merely because the user describes the camera as uncomfortable.

Recorded browser evidence in `VALIDATION.md`: ordinary Bridgehead launch, bike about one-fifth of the viewport, visible direct gate, and a drag that changes view and spends two rounds. Earlier default Ridge UI checks also exercised drag, keyboard taps, pause, resume and reload. They did not sustain movement or complete a route. Real pointer capture was rejected under automation; that proves a recoverable automated permission failure, not broken capture for a human.

The historical `gamep32.mov` is documented as a 17.88-second older graphics recording in `VALIDATION.md`. It is not a recording of this Bridgehead build, and was not newly inspected for this report. Generated chase-camera boards are design intentions, not gameplay evidence. Current source/recorded screenshots cannot establish motion comfort.

## What the smoke actually bypasses

`src/PlaytestPanel.tsx`, `bridgeheadSmoke`, directly sets `mission.previous`, player position, velocity and boost multiplier to cross entry and exit planes. This tests swept gate/state logic, not steering, altitude correction, route discovery, collision clearance or missed-gate recovery. It pauses while the enemy GLB loads, hiding the ordinary loading/threat transition.

Before combat it moves the player to `(12,2,-10)`. Every frame it computes a look-at quaternion and applies look rotation **eight times**, then invokes ordinary weapon fire. Real damage, ammunition and cooldown are valuable evidence; acquisition, sensitivity, regripping, tracking while moving, target occlusion and aiming under threat are bypassed. Direct animation-state assignments prove named clips are available, not that a human sees the telegraph early enough or understands recovery. Moving directly to extraction skips finding and braking into the beacon. Forced lethal damage and direct mission calls test terminal lifecycle, not a survivable sortie.

The 112 frame waits at exact 1280×720 renderer pixels (30.8 ms median, 33.0 ms p95) cover scripted fights, not a sustained natural flight workload. They are not input-to-photon latency, a GPU diagnosis, or a 60 FPS pass. The ≤20 ms p95 target remains unmet in that sample; foreground-browser profiling must separate game cost from automation scheduling.

## Capture setup and prohibited shortcuts

Use an ordinary foreground desktop browser and physical mouse/keyboard. Record commit plus dirty diff/build bundle identifier, URL, browser, OS, GPU actually used, mouse DPI/OS settings, sensitivity/invert, input mode, viewport, renderer dimensions, quality, and audio level. Use a clean test profile for defaults without overwriting the owner's saved settings. Capture at 1280×720 **renderer** pixels; record CSS size and DPR separately. Record continuous video with visible HUD and, if available, a keyboard/mouse overlay. Keep the game focused. Obtain player consent before retaining recordings.

No console transform writes, synthetic look-at, direct component calls, modified health/speed, QA autopilot or pausing to await assets. Read-only local telemetry is permitted, clearly labeled. If the automation tool cannot hold movement keys, mark that stage blocked and hand the physical-input portion to a human; taps or teleports are not substitutions. Run known-input engineering checks first, then a separate uncoached group. Give fresh players only: “Start a run, choose your route, defeat the threat and leave. You can use what the game shows you.” Do not reveal this protocol's coordinates to them.

## Staged ordinary-input recording

| Stage | Physical action and capture | Objective pass/fail proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Launch | Cold start; choose capture. Repeat in drag mode, including a deliberate capture denial if reproducible. Capture first playable frame and two seconds stationary. | Both start choices visible; rejection recoverable; no input required outside described controls. No unexplained initial camera jump or automatic movement/fire. |
| Turn | Look up/down, turn 180 degrees and return. In drag mode use right button, release/recenter, move over UI, return to canvas. Repeat while W is held; then release all input. | Zero horizon roll; bike does not pitch with aim; no motion from unheld cursor; no stale reentry jump or UI-triggered shot. No continued rotation after release beyond the next rendered update. Record drag count, angular gain and unintended shots. |
| Gate | Take low naturally on one attempt and high on another. Deliberately miss or reject high once and recover using only the displayed guidance. | Correct reward once, no premature Warden, no invisible boundary trap. Reviewer recovers within 15 seconds after understanding the cue; fresh-player recovery is recorded without coaching. Record every overshoot and altitude correction. |
| Bridge | Sustain movement across the selected line; capture midpoint and east landing without repositioning. Include a modest lateral correction. | Bike occupies proposed 15–25% screen height at four fixed capture points; passage/next target remains readable. No camera through declared solid cover, no proxy snag on the intended channel. Log visual-only rails separately, not as a solver pass. |
| Warden telegraph | Approach normally; stop firing long enough to observe two complete attack/recovery cycles. Capture enemy and HUD together. | Attack cue visible before damage and recovery visibly distinguishable. Record cue-onset, projectile launch, impact and recovery duration in video frames. Compare with configured timing; mapped clip names alone do not pass. |
| Dodge and aim | On the next cue, intentionally strafe or change altitude; then return aim and fire during recovery. Test both sides of real court cover. | Two intentional dodges out of three controlled trials, no damage before the telegraph, reciprocal cover. Record shots to first hit, time to reacquire, unintended firing and whether standing still is as safe/effective. These controlled trials are not fresh-player success counts. |
| Extraction | Find beacon after kill, fly there, brake, leave once during hold, then return and finish. | Target is findable within 10 seconds; progress resets on exit and completes after the displayed hold. No hidden position requirement, duplicate result or residual input on retry. |
| Interruption/retry | While moving/boosting/firing, Escape; resume each input mode. Repeat tab loss and ten full natural sorties, retaining failures. | No held action survives pause; reliable resume without refresh. No progressive resource growth after settling. Label these ten natural runs separately from existing forced-reset fixtures. |

For default Ridge, repeat launch/turn/pause and both complete routes with its own camera spacing. For `?full-level`, test only launch/turn/flight/stream transitions: it is a survey, not a combat or collision acceptance target.

## One release-to-next-iteration gate

Do not expand Bridgehead content until both input modes complete both routes using continuous ordinary input with zero control/camera blockers, and **at least four of five fresh players launch and make a first intentional hit without help**. Record discomfort, wrong-way recovery and voluntary replay separately; any reproducible severe camera defect blocks the gate even if the count passes. This is an internal small-sample usability gate, not premium certification. Then run the roadmap's fresh ten-player test and three declared foreground performance runs.

If this fails, choose the most repeated observed blocker, change one variable, rerun the identical engineering sequence, then recruit fresh players. Attach actual videos/screenshots and timestamps to `VALIDATION.md`; do not replace missing recordings with generated concepts. Input-to-photon latency requires suitable event/display instrumentation (or high-speed external capture), not inference from average frame intervals.
